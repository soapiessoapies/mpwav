Arranging panels: drag a panel by its handle, or use its Move and Place controls.

Playing box

Drag a clip to move it and its right edge to stretch it (the loop repeats). Double-click or double-tap a clip to edit its notes, or an empty spot to make a new clip. Click the ruler to choose where Play starts; drag along it to set the loop. With a keyboard: arrows move between clips, Alt + arrows move a clip, Shift + arrows stretch it, Enter opens it, Delete removes it.

Clip

    Tap in time with the beat, four times or more; the tempo follows your taps.

    When on, recording starts with one bar of clicks so you can come in on time.

    Clip

    How many bars this clip covers on the timeline; past its loop it repeats. It stops at the next clip.

    No clip open: double-click one, or press New clip.

    Notes

    Scroll up and down for higher and lower notes. Click an empty spot to add a note (drag right as you click to draw it longer); drag a note to move it and its right edge to stretch it; double-click a note to delete it. On a phone, tap to add. With a keyboard: arrows move the cursor (Ctrl a beat, Page Up/Page Down an octave), Enter adds or removes a note, Shift + arrows stretch it, Alt + arrows move it, Delete removes the selected notes. Space plays and stops from anywhere.

    Step input: play a key (on screen or on your computer keyboard) and that note goes in at the cursor, then the cursor moves on by the new-note length. Hold several keys for a chord. It's always on while the piano roll has focus.

    Record: the song keeps playing and whatever you play is added to the selected track at the bar that's playing (a new clip appears there if the track has none), snapped to the nearest step and as long as you held the key. Pick another track with [ ] or a click and keep playing to add to it too. Each pass adds on top; Undo take removes the last recording on every track.

    Fit to tempo speeds the sound up or slows it down so it fills the clip's loop exactly. Like a record, its pitch moves with it.

    Drag the edges of the bright part to trim, and the small squares at its top corners to fade. The fields above do the same from a keyboard.

    Note Edit

    This note's sound

    On top of the track's sound, for the selected notes only. Double-click a slider to put it back.

    Patterns

    Drum beats (added to this clip)
    Stamp a rhythm on the cursor row (C4)
    Arpeggio from the selected chord

    Number keys work too: 1–7 stamp a rhythm, 8 / 9 / 0 make an arpeggio up / down / up-and-down.

    Color

    Synth tracks play notes. Audio tracks play sound files (.wav, .mp3) as clips on the timeline: drop a file on a row, or press New clip on one.

    Key

    With a scale chosen, piano roll rows outside it are dimmed. Keep in key moves notes you place or drag onto the nearest note that fits; what you play and record stays as played.

    Swing plays every other sixteenth a little late, for a shuffled groove.

    A drum track plays the drum kit: kick, snare, clap, closed and open hat, two toms and a crash. Warp and Mix shape it; open a clip to place hits, or pick a beat on the Note page.

    An audio track plays sound files. Warp and Mix shape it; open one of its clips to trim, fade or reverse it.

    Instrument

    A sound file plays at its own pitch on that key, and higher or lower on the others. The Shape, Filter and Warp pages and each note's own sound still shape it.

    Morph

    Computer keys

    Plays the selected track. Tap or click the keys, or use your computer keys A W S E D … K (they work even with the keyboard hidden). Z and X change octave, Esc stops every note. On a focused key, arrows move and Enter plays. The chord buttons play a whole chord of the song's key from one press, and record like keys.

    Drum keys: A or C kick · S or D snare · D or E clap · F closed hat · G open hat · H or A low tom · J or B high tom · K or high C crash

        Settings

        Display

        Fills the whole screen with no browser bars. Esc or this button again leaves it; on Windows F11 does the same.

        Layout

        Auto picks from the window's width. Desktop always uses the side-by-side layout; Phone always stacks everything with the keyboard pinned at the bottom.

        Text and control size

        Auto grows the text with your screen, so it stays readable on a big monitor.

        Background

        High contrast is the default. Every background keeps all text and controls at full readability (WCAG AA).

        Look

        Pixel details (the default) draws sliders as pixel blocks, toggles as circles and Note Edit's pages as paper tabs. All pixel also squares off buttons, fields and clips. Smooth keeps soft, rounded controls.

        Tutorial

        A short walk through the studio, one part at a time. It runs once on your first visit.

        Tips are always read out by screen readers, even when they're hidden.

        App

        Keyboard

        Turn these off if you use speech input or tend to bump keys: then only Ctrl shortcuts work, and letters always play notes.

        Plug a MIDI keyboard in and turn this on: its keys play the selected track and record like the on-screen keys. The browser asks permission the first time.
